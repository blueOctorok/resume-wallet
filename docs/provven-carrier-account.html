Provven · Concept Brief

Lead with proof. Keep it simple.
For drivers and employers.

The driver's card is finished and trustworthy before anyone finds it. Because of that, the employer side can be almost nothing — a place to look, a way to say "I'm interested," and no to-do list.

Prepared September 29, 2026 · Internal · Companion to "Provven Access Model" brief

The idea in one paragraph

Provven's job is to give a driver the strongest, most trustworthy resume possible before any employer ever sees it — built from proof that costs the driver nothing. If we do that well, everything else gets simpler. Employers don't need to build anything with us; they show up, find a driver, and reach out. Paid steps like a driving-record check happen later, ordered by the employer, only when both sides are serious. We handle what matters at each step and nothing more.

Two rules for every decision in this brief: lead with the strongest free proof, and never ask a driver or an employer to do something that isn't needed yet.

Step 1 — The driver's card is built on free proof

Strongest first. Paid proof waits until an employer is serious.

Strong · Free
Verified employment. Provven confirms past employers directly and stamps the card. Driver-owned, shown to any employer the driver chooses. This is the opening move — it's what makes a stranger trust the card.
Good · Free
Driver's license on file (coming). Driver photographs their CDL; we read it and stamp the card "license on file." Honest label: a document the driver uploaded, one rung below Verified. Option: run it through a state-record license check (about a dollar each) and it becomes true third-party proof — the cheapest real verification in trucking.
Baseline · Free
The card itself. Experience, endorsements, equipment, references, DOT application — everything the driver enters, cleanly presented. Never labeled "verified."
Strong · Paid · Later
Driving record (MVR) and PSP. Ordered by the employer, with the driver's consent, when they're serious about one driver. Paid per order, right from the card. Every hiring company runs its own driving-record check anyway — the regulations require it — so this is never a cost of being found. A driver may buy their own to strengthen the card; we assume that's rare. Never shown publicly.
What the driver has to do: fill in the card, confirm past employers, snap a photo of their license. That's the whole to-do list. No payments, no paperwork they don't need yet.

Step 2 — Because the card is strong, the employer side can be tiny

Same login, same driver cards. Very different footprint.

Carrier Account Everyone else
  • Gets in: name, company, work email, agree to terms. Approved after a quick check.
  • Setup: none. No company profile, no tools to install, no team to manage.
  • Sees: Find Drivers, the driver's card, their own outreach and messages.
  • Can do: show interest / invite to apply, request more from a driver, message.
  • Pays: nothing to start. Per order if they ever run a screening.
  • Leaves: whenever. Comes back through a link in their email — no password.
Hub Pace · By invitation
  • Gets in: we set it up together.
  • Setup: full — company profile, team, screening tools, job postings.
  • Sees: dashboard, DQ file monitor, screening orders, applicants, jobs, team.
  • Can do: everything a Carrier Account can, plus order screenings in volume and run DQ files.
  • Pays: on volume.
  • Unchanged by this proposal.
Why we stop offering hubs to everyone: they're a hard sell before a carrier has found a single driver, they have many moving pieces, and drivers were ending up in employer accounts by mistake. Pace's hub works because we built it for them. If another carrier ever wants one, that's a human conversation — and we want it to be rare.

Step 3 — How a carrier actually uses it

Come in cold, look, act, leave, come back when something happens. Paid step is last and optional.

1

Discover

Clicks a driver's shared card link, or hits "Find drivers" on our homepage.

2

Quick sign-up

Name, company, work email, agree to terms. Approved after a quick check.

3

Find drivers

Lands straight on the search. Filters by state, CDL class, endorsements, experience.

4

Act

Show interest, invite to apply, request a missing piece, or message the driver.

5

Driver says yes

Verified employment and the full card unlock for that carrier.

6

Get serious

Order the MVR with consent, per order, from the card. Optional.

Why "come and go" works: email is the return path. When a driver accepts an invite or replies, the carrier gets an email with a link that drops them right back on that driver. They don't have to remember we exist — we remind them at the exact moment there's a reason to come back.

Guardrails we keep

Drivers stay in controlDrivers choose whether they can be found by employers, and what shows on their card. No driver is listed without opting in.
No driver-turned-employerAn email already registered as a driver can't open a Carrier Account. Personal email domains (Gmail, Yahoo…) go to manual review; company domains are approved quickly.
Screenings stay privateMVR and PSP results are never on a public link and never visible to a carrier who hasn't ordered them with consent.
Honest labelsOnly facts we confirmed with a third party wear "Verified." Uploaded documents say "on file." Self-entered information is never called verified.
Not a directoryWe don't advertise or sell a "database of drivers." Carriers find individuals and reach out one at a time; drivers decide who sees what.
Pace untouchedNothing about the Pace hub changes.

Decisions we'd like from you

  • License check. Worth eating roughly a dollar per driver to turn "license on file" into true verification on the free card?
  • Pricing. Free to look and reach out, pay only per screening order — or a modest fee for Carrier Accounts once they've made a few contacts?
  • Existing drivers. When we add the "let employers find me" switch, should current drivers default to on (and be told) or off (and be asked)?
